# Trademark and brand policy

**Short version:** the code is yours to use under the AGPL. The name is not.

The GNU AGPL v3.0 in [`LICENSE`](LICENSE) grants broad rights over the _source
code_ of Petits Signes. It grants no rights whatsoever over the project's name
or identity, and it was never intended to — free software licences cover
copyright, not trademarks. This document says plainly what that means here, so
that nobody has to guess.

## What is reserved

- The name **Petits Signes**, alone or as part of a longer name.
- The wordmark, the logo, the category icons as a set, and the visual identity
  as a whole. The logo's design also carries its own copyright, automatically
  and separately from this policy — a fact, not a request.
- The domain **petitsignes.cat** and any confusingly similar domain.

These have been in continuous public use as the identity of this project since
**July 2026**, verifiable in this repository's own commit history.

## What you may do

- Use, study, modify and redistribute the code under the AGPL, including
  running your own instance.
- State factually that your work is **based on**, **derived from** or
  **compatible with** Petits Signes, in ordinary descriptive prose.
- Link to petitsignes.cat, quote the project by name when referring to it, and
  write about it.
- Keep the attribution that the AGPL and CC BY-SA already require. That is not
  a trademark use; it is a licence obligation.

## What you may not do

- Name your fork, service, app or organisation **Petits Signes**, or anything
  close enough to be mistaken for it.
- Use the logo, wordmark or visual identity as the identity of your own work.
- Present your version in a way that suggests it is the official project, or
  that it is endorsed by, affiliated with or maintained by Petits Signes.
- Register the name, a similar name, or a similar domain as your own.

If you fork this project, **give it your own name**. That is the whole ask, and
it is also what the AGPL expects: your users should be able to tell whose
software they are running and who to hold responsible for it.

## Permission

Uses beyond the above need written permission. Ask first — for genuinely
non-commercial, educational or community uses the answer will usually be yes.

Open an issue at
<https://github.com/AlexandreMartinezOlmos/petitsignes/issues>.
